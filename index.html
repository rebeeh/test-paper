🔢 Number System Reference

Number Families

  • Natural Numbers (N): Counting numbers starting from 1: 1, 2, 3, 4, ...
  • Whole Numbers (W): Natural numbers together with zero: 0, 1, 2, 3, ... (0 is a whole number, but NOT a natural number).
  • Integers (Z): Positive whole numbers, zero, and negative whole numbers: ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
  • Rational Numbers (Q): Numbers that can be written in the form p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0 (e.g., 0.75, -3/4, 5 = 5/1).
  • Irrational Numbers: Numbers that cannot be written as simple fractions. Their decimals are non-terminating and non-repeating (e.g., √2, √3, π). Note that √9 = 3 is rational.

Divisibility Rules

  • Divisible by 2: Last digit is even (0, 2, 4, 6, or 8).
  • Divisible by 3: The sum of all digits is divisible by 3.
  • Divisible by 5: Last digit is 0 or 5.
  • Divisible by 9: The sum of all digits is divisible by 9.
  • Divisible by 10: Last digit is strictly 0.
Hierarchy: Natural numbers ⊂ Whole numbers ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational numbers ⊂ Real numbers.

🧮 Factors & Multiples Reference

Key Definitions

  • Factor: A number that divides another number completely without leaving a remainder. For example, factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.
  • Multiple: The result of multiplying a number by an integer. Multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, ...
  • Prime Number: A positive integer greater than 1 having exactly two factors: 1 and itself (e.g., 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, ...).
  • Composite Number: A positive integer greater than 1 that has more than two factors (e.g., 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, ...).
  • Special Case: The number 1 has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite. 2 is the only even prime number.

Finding Factors in Pairs

List factors systematically from 1 upwards:

Example for 24: 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8, 4 × 6 → Factors: {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24}.

🔢 Prime Factorisation Reference

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be uniquely expressed as a product of prime factors (disregarding the order of the primes).

Methods of Prime Factorisation

  1. Factor Tree: Split the number into any two factor branches and continue branching composite factors until only prime leaves remain.
  2. Ladder / Repeated Division: Divide the number repeatedly by the smallest prime factor that divides it evenly until the quotient is 1.

Exponent / Index Notation

Repeated prime factors are written using exponents:

  • 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2³ × 3
  • 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 2² × 3 × 5
  • 120 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 2³ × 3 × 5

➗ HCF & LCM Reference

Highest Common Factor (HCF / GCF)

The largest number that divides into two or more numbers with no remainder. Useful for dividing items into equal groups as large as possible.

Prime Factor Rule for HCF: Take only the common prime factors raised to their lowest power.

Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)

The smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. Useful for finding when repeating cycles, bells, or traffic lights coincide.

Prime Factor Rule for LCM: Take every prime factor that appears, raised to its highest power.

Key Relationship for Two Numbers

For any two positive integers a and b:

a × b = HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b)

Worked Example (12 and 18)

  • 12 = 2² × 3¹
  • 18 = 2¹ × 3²
  • HCF = 2¹ × 3¹ = 6
  • LCM = 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = 36
  • Verification: 12 × 18 = 216 = 6 × 36.
