🔢 Number System — Aptitude Cheat Sheet

1. Number Classifications

  • Natural Numbers (ℕ): Counting numbers {1, 2, 3, 4, ...}.
  • Whole Numbers (ℤ0): {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}. Note: 0 is whole, but NOT natural.
  • Integers (ℤ): {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}.
  • Rational Numbers (ℚ): Form p/q (where q ≠ 0 and p, q ∈ ℤ). Decimals either terminate (e.g. 1/4 = 0.25) or repeat infinitely (e.g. 1/3 = 0.333...).
  • Irrational Numbers: Non-terminating, non-repeating decimals (e.g. √2, √3, π). Note: √9 = 3 and 22/7 are rational.

2. Fast Divisibility Tests (Aptitude Shortcuts)

  • Divisibility by 2: Last digit is even {0, 2, 4, 6, 8}.
  • Divisibility by 3: Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
  • Divisibility by 4: Number formed by last two digits is divisible by 4 (e.g., 7324).
  • Divisibility by 5: Last digit is 0 or 5.
  • Divisibility by 8: Number formed by last three digits is divisible by 8 (e.g., 5120).
  • Divisibility by 9: Sum of all digits is divisible by 9.
  • Divisibility by 10: Units digit is 0.
  • Divisibility by 11: Difference between (Sum of digits at odd places) and (Sum of digits at even places) is 0 or a multiple of 11.

3. Recurring Decimals to Fractions Trick

Pure recurring: 0.aaaa... = a / 9
Two digits: 0.abab... = ab / 99
Mixed: 0.abcc... = (abc - ab) / 900
Aptitude Fact: Every prime number greater than 3 can be written in the form 6k ± 1 (where k is a positive integer). The converse is not necessarily true (e.g., 25 = 6(4)+1 is composite).

🔢 Prime Factorisation — Aptitude Cheat Sheet

1. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be uniquely factored into a product of prime powers:

N = p1a1 × p2a2 × ... × pkak

2. Coprime Numbers (Relatively Prime)

  • Two integers a and b are coprime if their HCF(a, b) = 1.
  • They do not need to be prime individually (e.g., 8 and 9 are both composite, but coprime).
  • Any two consecutive integers n and n + 1 are always coprime.

3. Counting Trailing Zeroes Shortcut

A trailing zero is produced by a factor of 10 = 2 × 5.
In any product or factorial, the number of trailing zeroes equals min(power of 2, power of 5). Since 2s are typically more abundant, count the power of 5.

4. Standard Prime Factorisations to Memorize

  • 60 = 2² × 3 × 5
  • 100 = 2² × 5²
  • 120 = 2³ × 3 × 5
  • 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5
  • 1001 = 7 × 11 × 13 (very common CAT/Banking number)

➗ HCF & LCM — Aptitude Cheat Sheet

1. Definitions & Prime Power Rules

  • HCF (Highest Common Factor): Product of common prime factors with their lowest exponents.
  • LCM (Lowest Common Multiple): Product of all prime factors with their highest exponents.

2. Two-Number Product Property

Product of two numbers = HCF × LCM
A × B = HCF(A, B) × LCM(A, B)

Warning: This product identity is valid strictly for two numbers. It does NOT hold for three or more numbers.

3. HCF & LCM of Fractions

HCF(a/b, c/d) = HCF(a, c) / LCM(b, d)
LCM(a/b, c/d) = LCM(a, c) / HCF(b, d)

Note: Ensure fractions are reduced to lowest terms before applying the formula.

4. Classic Aptitude Application Models

  • Simultaneous Bells / Traffic Lights / Circular Tracks: When will events coincide next? → Time = LCM(individual cycle times).
  • Equal Distribution / Largest Size: Maximum capacity of measuring vessel or greatest number of identical packs with no remainder → HCF(quantities).
  • Tiling a rectangular floor with minimum square tiles:
    Tile Side = HCF(Length, Width)
    Number of Tiles = (Length × Width) / [HCF(Length, Width)]²

🧮 Factors & Multiples — Aptitude Cheat Sheet

1. Total Number of Factors Formula

If the prime factorisation of N = pa × qb × rc, then:

Total Factors T(N) = (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1)

Example: 72 = 23 × 32 → Factors = (3 + 1)(2 + 1) = 4 × 3 = 12 factors.

2. Odd and Even Factors

  • Odd Factors: Ignore the power of 2: (b + 1)(c + 1). For 72: (2 + 1) = 3 odd factors (1, 3, 9).
  • Even Factors: a × (b + 1)(c + 1). For 72: 3 × 3 = 9 even factors.

3. Perfect Square Rule

Crucial Aptitude Rule: A number has an odd number of factors if and only if it is a perfect square (e.g., 36 has 9 factors; 25 has 3 factors). All non-square numbers have an even number of factors.

4. Pairs of Factors

The number of ways to write N as a product of two factors:

For non-squares: T(N) / 2
For perfect squares: [T(N) + 1] / 2
